Sample Subway – Nuclear Pressure Tube Sampling System

An embedded remote-controlled device engineered for Canadian Nuclear Laboratories (CNL) to retrieve and weigh 30–80 mg metal samples within 4-inch internal diameter, 15-foot nuclear pressure tubes.

View Details Gallery

Overview

The "Sample Subway" is an embedded electromechanical device engineered for Canadian Nuclear Laboratories (CNL) to scrape and extract 30–80 mg metal samples from the interior of 4-inch diameter, 15-foot nuclear pressure tubes. The collected samples undergo destructive testing to measure hydrogen embrittlement levels—a critical indicator of reactor core structural integrity.

The system operates autonomously within radiation zones to eliminate human exposure. It integrates an onboard strain-gauge load cell and 24-bit ADC for real-time sample mass verification during extraction, paired with wireless telemetry and a self-contained battery power supply.

System Specifications

  • Processor: ESP32-WROOM-C3 (C++ / Arduino Firmware)
  • Power Subsystem: Custom 3S1P Li-ion (Molicel 21700 P42A, 12V nominal), 3S BMS, LM2596 Buck Regulator
  • Actuation & Motor Control: BTS7960 High-Power H-Bridge Driver, JGB37-520 12V High-Torque Motor
  • Sensor Integration: 100g Load Cell with HX711 24-bit ADC module (real-time sample mass feedback)

Engineering Methodology

1. Technical Constraints & Requirements

The system was designed around rigid physical, operational, and environmental boundaries specified by CNL:

Geometry Constraints

4-inch internal tube diameter envelope, 15-foot insertion depth limit, and dual-orientation (horizontal/vertical) operational stability.

Target Payload

Precise mechanical extraction of 30–80 mg metal samples with real-time mass verification and target measurement accuracy of ±9.5 mg.

Power & Remote Control

Self-contained battery power delivery eliminating trailing cables, with wireless serial/Bluetooth status communication for remote operation.

2. Electronics & Power Architecture

The electrical system architecture balances high peak current motor loads with sensitive low-voltage analog mass measurement:

  • Power Distribution Tree: Unregulated 12V battery bus driving the BTS7960 motor driver for high-torque scraping, stepped down via LM2596 buck converter to 5V/3.3V for logic rails.
  • Analog Signal Chain: Strain gauge Wheatstone bridge paired with HX711 differential 24-bit ADC for low-mass measurement.
  • Fail-Safe & Recovery: Integrated winch mechanical tether and firmware stop/return routines for loss-of-power recovery.

3. Hardware Integration & Prototyping

Physical hardware assembly focused on point-to-point wiring validation, motor torque testing, load cell calibration routines, and assembly of high-discharge 21700 battery cells with integrated BMS protection.

4. Testing, Failure Analysis & PCB Design Roadmap

System testing and post-mortem evaluation identified key hardware failure modes and formed a revision engineering roadmap:

Root Cause Failure Analysis

Load cell calibration verified target mass detection, but high-torque motor stalls during pipe engagement caused current spikes that tripped the BMS protection circuit.

Hardware Improvement Roadmap

Transition from discrete breakout modules and point-to-point wiring to a single integrated 2-layer custom PCB.

Mechanical Safety Integration

Add a mechanical break-line to the lathe bit holder to prevent destructive motor stalls in pipe bound conditions.
